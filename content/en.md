Then in 2019 Macron looked at American unpredictability and famously declared NATO was experiencing **“brain death.”** Germany publicly rejected that characterization. [Euronews: Macron says NATO is suffering “brain death”](https://www.euronews.com/2019/11/07/frances-macron-says-nato-suffering-brain-death-questions-us-commitment)

# And now, October 2026

The relationship hasn't broken.

That's the important part.

NATO still exists. Article 5 still exists. American forces remain in Europe. European allies and the U.S. continue extensive military cooperation. NATO's Supreme Allied Commander said just last week that America will continue providing important capabilities, although increasingly **“critical but more limited”** ones as Europeans assume more responsibility. [Reuters: NATO steps up intelligence-sharing over Russia threat](https://www.reuters.com/world/nato-steps-up-intelligence-sharing-over-russia-threat-top-commander-says-2026-09-24/)

But psychologically, the relationship has changed.

And **that is basically how we arrived at this bizarre moment**.

It isn't really 🇺🇸 **vs** 🇫🇷🇩🇪 in the traditional enemy sense.

It's more like a **70-year alliance renegotiating its marriage while Russia is throwing rocks through the windows, China is opening a competing supermarket across the street, Ukraine is screaming for ammunition from the kitchen, and everyone is simultaneously arguing about who pays the electricity bill.**

The diesel fight this week is another manifestation of that deeper problem, not the beginning of it. Europe wants the ability to say **no** to Washington without becoming strategically helpless. Washington wants Europe to carry much more of its own burden, while still maintaining a strong U.S.-led alliance.

Those two objectives are compatible in theory.

The comedy of the last ten years is everyone discovering that **European independence has consequences once Europe actually starts trying to build it.**
